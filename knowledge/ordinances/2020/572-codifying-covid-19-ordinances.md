# MUNICIPAL ORDINANCE NO. 15-2020

AN ORDINANCE CODIFYING ENACTED ORDINANCES RELATIVE TO COVID-19, IMPOSING UNIFORM PENALTIES THEREOF, AND FOR OTHER
PURPOSES

Introduced by: Hon. Cesar M. Loyola and Hon. Bolivar I. Babila
Joint Committees on Health, Energy, & Environmental Protection and Peace & Order & Public Safety

## EXPLANATORY NOTE

*WHEREAS*, on March 16, 2020, President Rodrigo Roa Duterte declared a state of calamity throughout the Philippines
and imposed an Enhanced Community Quarantine throughout Luzon due to Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19);

*WHEREAS*, Municipal Ordinance No. 04-2020 was enacted on April 14, 2020, imposing curfew hours between 8pm-5am within
the Municipality;

<!-- page 6 -->

*WHEREAS*, on same date, Municipal Ordinance No. 06-2020 was likewise passed mandating the wearing of face mask or
other protective equipment by all persons in public areas during the enhanced community quarantine;

*WHEREAS*, on April 21, 2020, Municipal Ordinance No. 07-2020 was approved and passed prohibiting any form of
discrimination towards medical and non-medical frontline workers, and patients with confirmed, suspected, and
probable cases of COVID-19 in the Municipality;

*WHEREAS*, on April 30, 2020, Municipal Ordinance No. 09-2020 was enacted mandating the implementation of the "*Bayan
Mag-log sa Carmona COVID-19 Tracking System (BML-CCTS)*" of the Municipal Government of Carmona for purposes of
tracking and preventing the spread of coronavirus disease;

*WHEREAS*, Municipal Ordinance No. 10-2020 was passed on May 8, 2020, extending the penalties imposed under all
COVID-19 Municipal Ordinances during the implementation of the general community quarantine;

*WHEREAS*, on July 2, 2020, Municipal Ordinance No. 12-2020 was passed regulating the sale and/or consumption of
liquor or any alcoholic beverages within the Municipality of Carmona, Cavite during the general community quarantine
period;

*WHEREAS*, the Municipal Health Office reported that the number of COVID-19 cases in the Municipality continues to
rise and there is a need to strictly implement health safety protocols to all persons within the municipality,
whether resident or not;

*WHEREAS*, despite earnest efforts, stern warnings, constant pleas, preventive measures and stringent penalties in the
above Ordinances applied by the Municipal Government, some constituents showed defiance on the threat brought about
by the contagious disease;

*WHEREAS*, the above stated Ordinances prescribe different penalties for violation thereof;

*WHEREAS*, in view of the above premises, there is a need for a legislative action to prescribe uniform penalties for
violations of the said Ordinances to prevent confusion among the people and to ensure strict compliance of the
citizenry to the government guidelines and directives relative to the suppression and control of COVID-19 pandemic;

<!-- page 7 -->

*NOW, THEREFORE*, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE SANGGUNIANG BAYAN IN REGULAR SESSION THAT;

ARTICLE I
GENERAL PROVISIONS

## Section 1. Short Title

This Ordinance shall be known and cited as the "*CARMONA COVID-19 CODE*".

## Section 2. Declaration of Policy

It is hereby declared as a policy of the Municipal Government to exercise its powers, to take necessary and
appropriate actions for an efficient governance, to promote the general welfare of the people, to ensure safety and
public health. With this, the Municipal Government fully supports the National Government in all its efforts to
suppress and control the continuing spread and rise of COVID-19 cases.

## Section 3. Coverage

This Ordinance shall apply to all persons, whether resident or non-resident, and establishments within the
territorial jurisdiction of the Municipality of Carmona, Cavite during the implementation of Community Quarantine.

## Section 4. Definitions

For the purpose of this Code;

- a. Citation ticket - shall mean a ticket issued by a member of the Task Force to a person in violation of a
  prohibited act mentioned in Section 29 of this Code.
- b. Curfew Hours - shall mean the period from 8pm-5am of the following day, or as may be modified under this Code.
- c. Establishment - shall mean any residential, commercial, or industrial establishments with proper and valid
  permits duly issued operating and situated within the Municipality offering or selling commodities whether
  essential or not which includes but not limited to sari-sari stores, groceries, supermarkets, warehouses,
  production and manufacturing sites, and factories.
- d. Face mask - shall mean any protective mask used to cover the nose, mouth, and chin with specifications and
  proper use as recommended by the DOH (e.g. <!-- page 8 --> surgical mask, medical mask, or N95 mask) or any reusable
  or washable face mask ordinarily sold in commercial establishments.
- e. Liquor - shall include all types and kinds of alcoholic beverages being sold to public.
- f. Mass gathering - shall mean any group consisting of ten (10) or more persons doing any form of activity.
- g. Minimum Public Health Standards - shall mean guidelines set by the DOH and other government agencies to aid all
  sectors in all settings to implement non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPI), which refer to public health measures
  that do not involve vaccines, medicines, or other pharmaceutical interventions, which individuals and communities
  can carry out in order to reduce transmission rates, contact rates, and the duration of infectiousness in the
  population to mitigate COVID-19.
- h. Quarantine pass - shall mean the permit given to any resident to travel within the Municipality for purposes of
  buying essential products.
- i. Task Force - means the authorities created and directed through an Executive Order to strictly enforce this
  Code.
- j. Valid ID - means any identification where the name, address, birthday, picture and signature of the holder are
  visible.
- k. Violator - means any person violating any of the prohibited acts under this Code.

ARTICLE II
CURFEW HOURS

## Section 5. Curfew Hours

The curfew hours from 8:00 p.m. to 5:00 a.m. is hereby set and imposed within the territorial jurisdiction of the
Municipality of Carmona during the community quarantine.

<!-- page 9 -->

The above provision shall be subject to regular monitoring and re-assessment of the Carmona COVID-19 Inter-Agency
Task Force (Carmona IATF) and directives or proclamations issued by the National Government. The Municipal Mayor is
authorized to modify the curfew hours herein imposed upon the recommendation of the Carmona IATF.

## Section 6. Exemptions

The following shall be exempted from the implementation of the rule on curfew hours:

- (a) Hospital personnel such as doctors, nurses, and other health workers travelling for medical or humanitarian
  reasons;
- (b) Authorized government officials including peace and order personnel in both municipality and barangay levels as
  may be determined by the Local Chief Executive;
- (c) Those who work at night or night shift workers provided that they will show company I.D., certificate of
  employment or other supporting documents as proof of employment;
- (d) Delivery of basic necessities such as food and medicines;
- (e) Public transport services allowed by the Carmona IATF;
- (f) Emergency cases; and
- (g) Other exemptions that may be provided by the National Government and/or any competent authority.

ARTICLE III
LIQUOR BAN

## Section 7. Liquor Ban

The possession, consumption, sale, or delivery of beer, liquor, or any alcoholic beverages in any place within the
territorial jurisdiction of the Municipality of Carmona during the community quarantine is hereby prohibited.

The above provision shall be subject to regular monitoring and re-assessment of the Carmona IATF and directives or
proclamations issued by the National Government. The Municipal Mayor is authorized to lift and re-impose
implementation of the liquor ban upon the recommendation of the Carmona IATF.

<!-- page 10 -->

ARTICLE IV
MANDATORY WEARING OF FACE MASK

## Section 8. Mandatory Wearing of Face Mask or its Equivalent

It is hereby mandated the wearing of face masks or other protective equipment by all persons in public
areas/business establishments during the COVID-19 pandemic.

However, due to the prevailing shortage of supplies, wearing of improvised masks and other protective equipment, such
as indigenous, reusable, homemade or do-it-yourself (DIY) masks, face shields, and handkerchiefs, that can reduce
risks of local transmission of COVId-19, shall be allowed.

## Section 9. Business Establishments Compliance Requirements

All operational business establishments, whether commercial or industrial, shall not allow entry of their employees,
customers, or any person without wearing a face mask or an equivalent thereof in said establishment. Further, said
establishments shall provide a face mask for every employee and display the full copy of this Ordinance at or near
the entrance of their workplace.

ARTICLE V
SOCIAL DISTANCING PROTOCOLS

## Section 10. Social/Physical Distancing

This Ordinance is enacted to prescribe the Social/Physical Distancing protocol especially in places where significant
numbers of people are present. For this purpose, the following activities/operations shall not be allowed: mass
gatherings, celebrations, religious activities, *fiestas*, *karakols*, parades, wakes, sporting events, entertainment
activities, community assemblies, wellness centers, leisure parks, *tiangges*, sidewalk vending, computer gaming
shops, betting stations, casinos, cockpits, unauthorized operation of public utility vehicles, and the likes.

## Section 11. Back-riding of Couples on Motorcycles with Protective Barrier as an Exemption

Motorcycle back-riding shall be allowed for couples living together on the same household subject to the following
conditions:

- a. A protective barrier/shield whose design is duly approved by the National Task Force against COVID-19 must be
  placed between the driver and the back-rider;

<!-- page 11 -->

- b. Valid IDs or documents shall be presented as proof of marriage or relationship of the driver and the back-rider;
- c. Persons riding on motorcycles shall observe minimum public health standards set forth by the Department of Health
  such as wearing of masks and other relevant PPEs; and
- d. Riders shall observe all road-safety measures during their ride such as the donning of appropriate safety
  headwear or crash helmets.

However, the provisions under this Article shall be subject to regular monitoring, re-assessment, and modification of
the Carmona IATF and directives or proclamations issued by the National Government.

ARTICLE VI
HOME QUARANTINE PASS

## Section 12. Home Quarantine Pass (HQP) with QR Code

The HQP guidelines are as follows:

- (a) HQP with Quick Response (QR) code shall be issued for free to workers, employees, or any authorized member of the
  family per household allowing the bearer to go outside to purchase food, medicine, and other basic and essential
  necessities: Provided, that only one (1) HQP shall be given per household;
- (b) The bearer of HQP shall present his/her valid ID to ensure that HQP is not used by an unauthorized individual;
- (c) No issuance of HQP to all senior citizens, persons with disability (PWDs), pregnant women, minors, and people
  with serious medical conditions (such as diabetes, heart or kidney ailment) who are vulnerable and with high risk of
  death due to COVID-19 infection or complications;
- (d) Any person with no HQP with QR code and identification card (ID) shall not be allowed to enter commercial
  establishments;

<!-- page 12 -->

ARTICLE VII
ANTI-LOITERING

## Section 13. Anti-Loitering

Roaming around on public roads and streets without valid purpose or transaction, except for physical exercise, shall
not be allowed during the COVID-19 pandemic. All persons shall stay in their homes during the COVID-19 pandemic,
except those given HQP who may come out only to work, purchase food and medicines, throw garbage, clean their
backyard or surroundings, pay bills, seek medical consultations, and accompany an immediate family member to hospital
or clinic, provided proof of transaction to be made shall be presented.

ARTICLE VIII
ANTI-SPITTING AND ANTI-LITTERING

## Section 14. Anti-Spitting

No person shall spit mucus, saliva, phlegm, or other substances from the mouth, vomit, or openly sneeze out nasal
mucus or other substances from the nose on streets, alleys, sidewalks, parks, grocery stores, markets, shopping and
business centers, schools, hospitals, public carriers, public halls and buildings, parking spaces, terminals, and
other similar public places.

## Section 15. Anti-Littering

No person shall litter or improperly dispose of used or contaminated Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and/or face
mask, or defecate, or excrete bodily wastes or feces, or urinate on streets, alleys, sidewalks, parks, grocery
stores, markets, shopping and business centers, schools, hospitals, public carriers, public halls and buildings,
parking spaces, terminals, and other similar public places.

ARTICLE IX
ANTI-DISCRIMINATION

## Section 16. Anti-Discrimination

This Ordinance is enacted to protect medical and non-medical frontline workers as well as patients with confirmed,
suspected, and probable cases of COVID-19 from any form of discrimination.

The following acts or omissions are hereby declared unlawful:

<!-- page 13 -->

- (a) To commit any act or utterance which causes stigma, disgrace, shame, humiliation, harassment or otherwise
  discriminating against persons with confirmed, suspected, or probable cases of COVID-19 including public and
  private doctors, nurses, health workers, emergency personnel and volunteers, police, military, essential services
  personnel, and government/non-government social workers;
- (b) To refuse assistance or deny service to persons with confirmed, suspected, and probable cases of COVID-19, and to
  our frontline workers; and
- (c) To spread rumors, or utter information or any communication that causes unnecessary fear, anger, panic or
  violation of rights of persons above-mentioned.

ARTICLE X
CARMONA COVID-19 TRACKER

## Section 17. Carmona COVID-19 Tracker (Digital Contact Tracer)

There is hereby mandated the implementation of the "*Bayan Mag-Log sa* Carmona COVID-19 Tracking System (Digital
Contact Tracer) of the Municipal Government for the purposes of tracking and preventing the spread of the coronavirus
and flatten the curve of infections.

## Section 18. Limited Personal Information

Personal information of persons covered by this Ordinance shall be limited to full name, birth date, gender, address,
and contact number. Smartphone users shall be given a unique identification (ID) after signing up and shall be
required to log in once to the BML-CCTS when they go out of their residences and log every interaction made. However,
non-smartphone users shall be required to present their HQP or valid ID to PNP personnel/*barangay tanods* for the
purpose of logging in to the BML-CCTS. It is the responsibility of the IT Unit, in coordination with the Carmona
Municipal Police Station (CMPS) and the barangays, to collect and process the data every day.

## Section 19. Required Logging of Interactions

All persons shall be required to download/install on their smartphones (Android or IOS) and activate the Carmona
COVID-19 Tracker or Digital Contact Tracer (DCT) Application and log every interaction. It shall be unlawful for any
person to provide false information to the proper authorities about his/her identity, interactions, and/or movements
that may have posed significant risk to public health or resulted in the transmission or spread of COVID-19
infections.

<!-- page 14 -->

## Section 20. Mandatory Utilization of DCT Application and Posting of Business QR Codes

All business establishments within the Municipality shall be required to display their respective QR codes in plain
view at the place where the business is conducted and shall require their employees and customers to scan the said
QR code using the Carmona Digital Contact Tracer. In case an employee or customer has no smartphone nor downloaded
COVID-19 Tracker application, the management of the establishment shall log the required personal information of its
employee/customer.

ARTICLE XI
MANDATORY QUARANTINE IN A HOSPITAL OR GOVERNMENT ISOLATION FACILITY OF ALL PERSONS WITH CONFIRMED, SUSPECTED, AND
PROBABLE COVID-19 CASES

## Section 21. Mandatory Quarantine

All persons with COVID-19 confirmed and suspected cases including their close contacts shall be required to undergo
the necessary quarantine, isolation, and proper treatment in a hospital or government isolation facility to quell
the spread of COVID-19.

However, home quarantine, when circumstances warrant, may be permitted as a recourse for mild or asymptomatic positive
individuals only when they meet all of the following conditions:

- a. The patient has his/her own room;
- b. The patient has his/her own bathroom;
- c. There are no household members who are vulnerable or have co-morbidities (i.e. senior citizens, people with
  underlying health conditions, and pregnant women); and
- d. Approval of the Municipal Health Office.

## Section 22. Identification and Immediate Transfer of COVID-19 Positive Individuals

The conduct of house visitations for the identification and immediate transfer of COVID-19 positive individuals who
fail to meet the above-cited criteria shall be carried out by the Carmona Epidemiological Surveillance Unit (CESU) and
health workers including the concerned Barangay Health Emergency Response Team (BHERT), who may also call on the
Carmona Municipal Police Station (CMPS) police force for assistance when needed or warranted.

<!-- page 15 -->

## Section 23. Location-based Tracking System (PSC Tracker)

All persons confirmed with COVID-19 including their closed contacts shall also be required to: (a) install the
Location-based Tracking System (Probable Suspected Confirmed Tracker) developed by the IT Unit of the Municipal
Government; (b) ensure that their smartphones are always active on a 24/7 basis during their confinement/quarantine;
and (c) receive random calls from MHO anytime of the day or night. The Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and
Management Office (MDRRMO), in coordination with the concerned barangay, shall monitor on a 24/7 basis compliance
thereof.

The cellular phone issued by the Municipal Government shall be immediately returned once the confirmed COVID-19
individual or his/her close contact has been cleared by MHO from the coronavirus disease. In case of loss or damage,
the recipient thereof shall be liable to pay the sum of Three Thousand Five Hundred Pesos (PhP3,500.00).

ARTICLE XII
MINIMUM PUBLIC HEALTH STANDARDS

## Section 24. Minimum Public Health Standards

All business establishments, factories, and private/public institutions shall observe the Minimum Public Health
Standards set forth by the Department of Health to ensure COVID-19 mitigation, to wit:

- a. Objective 1: Increase physical and mental resilience
  - i. Ensure access to basic needs of individuals, including food, water, shelter and sanitation.
  - ii. Support adequate nutrition and diets based on risk.
  - iii. Encourage appropriate physical activity for those with access to open spaces as long as physical distancing
    is practiced.
  - iv. Discourage smoking and drinking of alcoholic beverages.
  - v. Protect the mental health and general welfare of individuals.
  - vi. Promote basic respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette.
  - vii. Protect essential workforce through provision of food, PPE and other commodities, lodging, and shuttle
    services as necessary.
  - viii. Provide financial and healthcare support for workforce who contracted COVID-19 through transmission at work.
  - ix. Limit exposure of most-at-risk population groups, such as through limitation in entry or prioritization in
    service or provision of support.
  - x. Provide appropriate social safety net support to vulnerable groups for the duration of the COVID-19 health
    event.

<!-- page 16 -->

- b. Objective 2: Reduce transmission
  - i. Encourage frequent hand washing with soap and water, and discourage the touching of the eyes, nose, and mouth,
    such as through appropriate information and education campaigns.
  - ii. Encourage individuals to stay at home unless there is a pressing need to go to a health facility for medical
    consultation or seek medical attention/confinement caused by other sickness/illness, if virtual consultation is
    not possible.
  - iii. Ensure access to basic hygiene facilities such as toilets, handwashing areas, water, soap, alcohol/sanitizer.
  - iv. Clean and disinfect the environment regularly, every two hours for highly touched areas such as toilets, door
    knobs, switches, and at least once every day for workstations and other surfaces.
  - v. Ensure rational use of PPE that is suitable to the setting, and the intended user. Medical-grade protective
    apparel shall be reserved for health care workers and other frontliners, and symptomatic individuals at all
    times.
- c. Objective 3: Reduce contact
  - i. Implement strict physical distancing (minimum of one meter) at all times, specially at public areas,
    workstations, eating areas, queues, and other high traffic areas.
  - ii. Reduce movement within and across areas and settings.
  - iii. Restrict unnecessary mass gatherings.
  - iv. Limit non-essential travel and activities.
  - v. Install architectural or engineering interventions, as may be deemed appropriate.
  - vi. Implement temporary closure or suspension of service in high risk areas or establishments, as necessary.
- d. Objective 4: Reduce duration of infection
  - i. Identify and immediately isolate symptomatic individuals, such as through the use of temperature scanning,
    symptom self-monitoring, and voluntary disclosure.
  - ii. Coordinate symptomatic individuals through appropriate health system entry points such as primary care
    facilities or teleconsulting platforms.
  - iii. Trace and quarantine close contacts of confirmed individuals consistent with Department of Health guidelines.

ARTICLE XIII
MANDATORY REPORTING OF COVID-19 CASES
BY BUSINESS AND MEDICAL ESTABLISHMENTS

## Section 25. Mandatory Reporting of COVID-19 Cases

Medical and business establishments and factories shall be required to report necessary information relating to
positive COVID-19 cases or other communicable diseases to enhance the contact-tracing efforts of the Municipal Health
Office. The Municipal Government through the Municipal Mayor or Municipal Health Officer may enter into a
data-sharing agreement with any medical institutions in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 or the Data Privacy
Act. In addition thereto, the said establishments shall be required to coordinate with the MHO regarding their
conduct of swab testing and to declare the results thereof.

## Section 26. Compliance to Health Declaration Form

All establishments shall be required to comply with the Health Declaration Protocol including the weekly reporting of
the Health Declaration Form to MHO. Disclosure of truthful personal information and the filling out of a Health
Declaration Form shall be required upon any person entering any establishments, where information shall be limited to
name, date and time accomplished, temperature, name, age, gender, complete address, contact information, health
conditions/manifestation of symptoms, attendance to any gathering or event, and travel history.

ARTICLE XIV
MANDATORY DECLARATION OF TRUTHFUL INFORMATION

## Section 27. Mandatory Declaration of Truthful Information

Any person who shall be required by any medical clinic, hospital, health center/office, diagnostic center,
laboratory, or any health/medical facility, whether public or private, to fill-out or accomplish health declaration
form or any similar document, shall be expected to observe honesty, state the truth, and disclose all relevant
information as may be required in the said form or document or as may be asked by the health worker, hospital
employee, or concerned public officer such as, but not limited to nurse, medical officers, or quarantine officers.

ARTICLE XV
DISCIPLINE AS THE NEW NORMAL

<!-- page 18 -->

## Section 28. Individual Discipline Prescribed as the New Normal

Individual discipline is prescribed as the new normal to combat COVID-19 in the Municipality of Carmona. This includes
following health safety protocols like wearing a face mask in public places and workplaces, sanitizing hands
regularly, observing one-meter physical distancing and cough etiquette, avoiding confined and closed spaces with poor
ventilation and crowded places, increasing physical and mental resilience, and staying at home for minors and senior
citizens, among other things.

ARTICLE XVI
PROHIBITED ACTS

## Section 29. Prohibited Acts

It is hereby ordered that the following acts are prohibited and violation hereof is penalized:

- a. Seen outside the residence during curfew hours;
- b. No face mask or not wearing the face mask properly;
- c. Travelling without a quarantine pass;
- d. Loitering;
- e. Spitting/Littering;
- f. Non observance of social distancing;
- g. Mass gathering;
- h. Violation of Motorcycle Back-riding Protocol;
- i. No QR Code/Non-usage of the Covid-19 Tracker Application;
- j. Non-compliance with the Location-based Tracking System (PSC Tracker) protocols;
- k. Selling, buying, or bringing liquor;
- l. Possessing and drinking liquor;
- m. Violation of Mandatory Quarantine;
- n. Untruthful declaration/non-declaration of required information;
- o. Non-declaration of Swab test results;
- p. Non-coordination of swab testing and results;
- q. Non-compliance by establishments with health declaration form; and
- r. Non-compliance by establishments/institutions with the Minimum Public Health Standards.

ARTICLE XVII
PENAL PROVISIONS

## Section 30. Penalties

Any person or establishment in violation of the prohibited acts mentioned in Article XVI shall be given citation
ticket for every offense and in addition thereof, be imposed with the following penalties:

A. FOR INDIVIDUALS:

- a. First (1st) offense - Any person caught in violation of any of the prohibited acts shall be meted a penalty of
  fine of ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS (PhP 1,500.00) and/or Community Service of at least THREE (3) days but not
  more than FIVE (5) days.
- b. Second (2nd) offense - Any violator caught for the second time shall be imposed a penalty of fine of TWO
  THOUSAND PESOS (PhP 2,000.00) and/or Community service of not less than FIVE (5) days but not to exceed TEN (10)
  days.
- c. Third (3rd) offense and oftener - Any violator caught for the third time or oftener, shall be meted a penalty of
  fine of TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS (PhP 2,500.00) and/or IMPRISONMENT OF NOT LESS THAN TWO (2) MONTHS BUT NOT
  MORE THAN SIX (6) MONTHS upon discretion of the court.

B. FOR ESTABLISHMENTS

- a. First (1st) offense - Any establishment found to have violated any of the prohibited acts shall be meted a fine
  of ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS (PhP 1,500.00) and SUSPENSION OF BUSINESS PERMIT FOR ONE (1) MONTH.
- b. Second (2nd) offense - Any establishment found to have violated any of the prohibited acts for the second time
  shall be meted a fine of TWO THOUSAND PESOS (PhP 2,000.00) and SUSPENSION OF BUSINESS PERMIT FOR TWO (2) MONTHS.
- c. Third (3rd) offense - Any establishment found to have violated any of the prohibited acts for the third time
  shall be meted a fine of TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS (PhP 2,500.00) AND REVOCATION OF BUSINESS PERMIT WITH
  CLOSURE.

<!-- page 20 -->

In the case of corporations, partnerships or associations, the president, general manager, or person-in-charge of
such establishment shall be held liable therefor.

C. FOR MINORS AND INDIVIDUALS ABOVE 60 YEARS OF AGE

- a. First (1st) offense - If the minor was found to have violated any of the prohibited acts, the minor shall be
  referred to the barangay hall where the minor resides, or if the minor is not a resident, to the nearest barangay
  hall to be handled by the Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC) as focal person. Thereafter, the
  parent/guardian shall be summoned and informed of the infraction and apprehension including the details thereof.

  The BCPC shall conduct a discussion or counseling with the minor together with the parent/guardian.

  Before turning over the minor, the parent/guardian shall be informed of the consequences they will be dealing with
  in case of subsequent violation of the minor pursuant to the applicable provisions under this Code.

  Further, the parent/guardian of the minor shall pay a penalty of fine amounting to ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS
  (PhP 1,500.00) and render TWENTY-FOUR (24) HOURS OF COMMUNITY SERVICE.
- b. Second (2nd) offense - If the minor was found to have violated any of the prohibited acts for the second time,
  the minor shall undergo the same procedure as provided for under the First (1st) offense.

  Further, the parent/guardian of the minor shall pay a penalty of TWO THOUSAND PESOS (PhP 2,000.00) and render
  THIRTY-SIX (36) HOURS OF COMMUNITY SERVICE.
- c. Third (3rd) offense and oftener - If the minor violated any of the prohibited acts for the third time and
  oftener, the minor shall, in addition to the conditions set forth in the preceding paragraphs, be turned over to
  the Municipal Social Welfare and Development (MSWD) Officer to undergo Intervention programs prescribed by Republic
  Act 9344, otherwise known as the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006.

<!-- page 21 -->

  Further, the parent/guardian of the minor shall pay a penalty of fine amounting to TWO THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED PESOS
  (PhP 2,500.00) and render FORTY-EIGHT (48) HOURS OF COMMUNITY SERVICE.

  The minor and the parent/guardian shall submit a Certificate of Compliance duly issued by the MSWD Officer stating
  among others that the fine had been paid showing proof thereof and that the parent/guardian had already completed
  the number of hours of community service.

  If the offender is an individual of 60 years and above, the maximum penalty of fine prescribed herein shall be
  imposed in any stage of offense.

D. ADDITIONAL PENALTIES

Failure of the violator to pay the fine herein imposed, he/she shall be disqualified from receiving assistance and
other benefits from the Barangay/Municipal Government including the issuance of barangay or police clearance for a
period of six (6) months reckoning from the time when the violation was committed and until he/she paid the said
fine.

The imposition of the foregoing penalties shall not exclude the filing of criminal, civil, or administrative
complaint prescribed under existing laws, rules and regulations against the violator.

E. LIQUOR CONFISCATION

The confiscated items shall be destroyed within fifteen (15) days from date of confiscation in the presence of the
CMPS Chief of Police or his/her representative, SB Committee Chair on Peace and Order and Public Safety, and a member
of Carmona IATF.

F. MANNER OF PAYMENT OF THE PENALTY

The violator shall pay the corresponding fine/s within five (5) working days from the issuance of the citation
ticket.

ARTICLE XVIII
FINAL PROVISIONS

## Section 31. Appropriation

The necessary amount for the effective enforcement of this Code is hereby appropriated which shall be taken from the
Annual Local Expenditure Program (Annual Budget), subject to the usual accounting and auditing procedures.

## Section 32. Implementation

The duly created Task Force is hereby directed to strictly enforce and implement this Code. The proceeds of this
Ordinance shall be used to help cure those infected by COVID-19. To ensure the effective enforcement of this Code, the
Carmona IATF may issue supplemental guidelines and rules and regulations, subject to the approval of the Municipal
Mayor.

## Section 33. Information Dissemination

The Municipal Information, Tourism, Culture and Arts Office (MITCAO) is hereby ordered to immediately inform the
public upon effectivity of this Ordinance.

## Section 34. Repealing Clause

All ordinances, executive orders, issuances and rules and regulations or parts thereof, which are inconsistent or in
conflict with any of the provisions of this Ordinance is hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

## Section 35. Separability Clause

If any of the provisions contained in this Ordinance is declared by a competent court to be unconstitutional or
invalid, the provisions which are not declared shall remain in full force and effect.

## Section 36. Effectivity Clause

This Ordinance shall immediately take effect upon compliance with the mandatory posting requirement prescribed by the
Local Government Code of 1991.
